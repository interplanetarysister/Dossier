# Proposal structure

Write for the specified recipient and purpose. Use clear headings, short paragraphs, concrete benefits and terminology appropriate to the business field. Professional style describes writing quality, not fonts, designed pages, embedded images, charts or PDF rendering.

Select supported sections from this sequence:

1. Title naming the offering and intended recipient.
2. Executive summary with the strongest documented benefits.
3. Customer need or opportunity, limited to evidence and user-provided context.
4. Offering: products or services, scope, delivery and supported features.
5. Advantages: documented differentiators; competitor names only when sourced and relevant.
6. Commercial details: supplied prices, currencies, units, inclusions, exclusions and validity dates.
7. Delivery and next step: use supplied commitments; an unspecified next step is an invitation, not an invented promise.
8. Source notes: labels, titles and dates where known, with available page or section locators.

Omit unsupported optional sections. Ask about missing essential details. Adapt service proposals toward scope and deliverables, product proposals toward specifications and benefits, and partnership proposals toward documented contributions. Never invent clients, partnerships, testimonials, certifications, forecasts or guarantees.

Keep source paths in the user's local evidence notes. Do not publish or externally transmit documents or private paths. External sharing is outside this skill.
